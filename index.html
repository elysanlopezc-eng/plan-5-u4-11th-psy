CNB Competence & Learning Goal

Competence: Differentiates the foundational psychological perspectives, analyzing their principles to explain human behavior and mental processes in real-world contexts.

Learning Goal: Students will successfully categorize and compare 4 key psychological perspectives (Biological, Behavioral, Cognitive, Psychodynamic) through a test anxiety case analysis.

Montano Habits & Core Skills

Habit 5: "Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood" (Viewing behavior through multiple lenses before judging).

Key Skills: Critical Thinking, Analytical Reasoning, Communication, Emotional Intelligence, and Collaborative Problem-Solving.

Assessment & Resources

Evaluation: Diagnostic (Intro warm-up), Formative (Group matrix application), Summative (Plenary Rubric).

Resources: Whiteboard, Case Study Cards, Perspective Handouts, Interactive Quiz, Rubric.

Moment I: Introduction & Case Presentation

10 Minutes

Objective: Hook student interest, activate prior knowledge, and introduce the central real-world scenario of Test Anxiety.

Case Study: "Alex's Exam Panic"

"Alex is a high school student who studies thoroughly for every psychology exam. However, as soon as the test papers are handed out, Alex's heart begins to race violently, hands start sweating, and their mind goes completely blank. Alex begins thinking: 'I’m going to fail, I always ruin everything.' Alex remembers being harshly criticized by a parent in 3rd grade after failing a math quiz and now feels an overwhelming urge to flee the room whenever an exam starts."

Brainstorm Prompt for Students: Why is Alex experiencing this? Is it a physical reaction? A learned habit? Negative self-talk? Or deeper unconscious fears?

Quick Class Poll: What is the PRIMARY cause of Alex's anxiety?

*Teacher Note: Click options as students raise hands to record votes live on the board!*

Moment II: The 4 Key Psychological Perspectives

20 Minutes

Explore the fundamental concepts, key focus areas, and prominent theorists of each psychological perspective.

1. Biological Perspective

Brain, Genetics, & Physiological Systems

  • • Core Focus: How brain anatomy, neurotransmitters, hormones, and genes drive behavior.
  • • Key Keywords: Nervous system, fight-or-flight, cortisol, adrenaline, genetics.
  • • Key Theorists: Roger Sperry, Paul Broca.
"Behavior is a physical product of biological processes. To change feelings or behavior, we must examine physiological reactions and brain chemistry."

2. Behavioral Perspective

Observable Actions & Learned Responses

  • • Core Focus: How environmental stimuli, conditioning, rewards, and punishments shape actions.
  • • Key Keywords: Conditioning, reinforcement, stimulus, response, habits.
  • • Key Theorists: B.F. Skinner, John B. Watson, Ivan Pavlov.
"Psychology should focus only on observable behavior. Behaviors are learned through conditioning and association with environmental consequences."

3. Cognitive Perspective

Mental Processes, Memory, & Thinking

  • • Core Focus: How people process, store, retrieve, and interpret information.
  • • Key Keywords: Perception, memory, beliefs, problem-solving, cognitive distortions.
  • • Key Theorists: Jean Piaget, Aaron Beck, Albert Ellis.
"It is not events themselves that upset us, but the way we interpret and think about those events in our minds."

4. Psychodynamic Perspective

Unconscious Drives & Early Childhood Traumas

  • • Core Focus: How unconscious conflicts, early childhood experiences, and hidden urges guide behavior.
  • • Key Keywords: Unconscious mind, defense mechanisms, repressed memories, inner conflict.
  • • Key Theorists: Sigmund Freud, Carl Jung, Alfred Adler.
"Behavior is driven by powerful inner forces and unresolved past memories operating deep below conscious awareness."

Moment III: Cooperative Application — "Analyzing Alex's Case"

20 Minutes

Instructions for Teams: Form groups of 4. Assign roles: 1) Leader/Facilitator, 2) Spokesperson, 3) Scribe/Secretary, 4) Timekeeper. Complete the matrix by applying each perspective to Alex's test anxiety.

Perspective Explanation of Alex's Test Anxiety Proposed Intervention / Solution Teacher Guidance / Reveal
Biological Overactivation of sympathetic nervous system, excess adrenaline release, genetic predisposition to high stress. Deep breathing exercises, beta-blockers, heart-rate regulation techniques.
Model Solution (Biological): Alex experiences an overactive autonomic fight-or-flight response. The brain perceives the exam as a physical threat, flooding the bloodstream with cortisol and adrenaline, causing racing heart and sweating.
Behavioral Conditioned fear response triggered by exam setting due to past negative reinforcement/punishment. Systematic desensitization, mock exams, positive reinforcement for remaining calm.
Model Solution (Behavioral): Alex learned to pair tests (conditioned stimulus) with anxiety/fear (conditioned response) due to past punishment after failing a quiz. The desire to flee is an avoidance behavior reinforced by temporary relief.
Cognitive Irrational belief systems, catastrophizing thoughts ("I will fail, I ruin everything"), memory overload. Cognitive reframing, replacing negative self-talk with realistic affirmations.
Model Solution (Cognitive): Alex engages in cognitive distortions (catastrophizing and overgeneralization). Negative automatic thoughts consume working memory capacity, causing Alex's mind to "go blank."
Psychodynamic Unconscious fear of disappointing parents stemming from repressed 3rd-grade childhood trauma. Psychoanalysis, uncovering hidden conflicts, building awareness of unconscious pressures.
Model Solution (Psychodynamic): Alex's panic is rooted in unconscious fear of rejection and repressed shame connected to childhood criticism. The test symbolizes a threat to self-worth and parental approval.
Montano Habit Integration: Habit 5 — Seek First to Understand

Just as psychologists must understand multiple perspectives before forming a diagnosis, practice listening deeply to your teammates' points of view during group discussions without interrupting!

Moment IV: Plenary & Interactive Knowledge Check

10 Minutes

Team Sharing Prompts (5 mins)

Prompt 1: "Team Spokespersons: How did your group's perspective differ from others in explaining Alex's panic?"
Prompt 2: "Can one single perspective explain human behavior completely, or do we need an eclectic approach?"

Interactive Quiz: "Perspective Matcher"

Score: 0 / 3

1. "Dr. Vance treats anxiety by asking clients to reframe automatic negative thoughts into positive affirmations." Which perspective is this?

2. "Sarah's fear of dogs began after being bitten at age five." Which perspective best explains this reaction?

3. "Unexplained mood swings are linked to hormonal imbalances in the thyroid gland." Which perspective is this?
